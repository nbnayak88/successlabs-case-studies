## Prologue — The Night the Energy System Stopped Talking
At a fictional OmniVerse refinery, an asset-health warning reaches the control room. Maintenance sees an old work order, procurement sees a delayed spare part, and the operations team lacks one reliable asset history. Elsewhere, a customer challenges outage information while renewable planners struggle to reconcile generation forecasts. None of these events is a real incident: they are simulation triggers for executive discovery.
**OmniVerse Energy Ltd.** is a fictional USD 85 billion energy group with 68,000 employees across 42 countries. Its integrated portfolio spans upstream oil and gas, refining, retail, power generation and renewables. The CEO's challenge is not merely to modernize IT; it is to transform how the business delivers safe, reliable and increasingly sustainable energy.
## Act I — Understand the Business (Master 01–04)
Fictional CEO **Dr. Asha Menon** assembles the leadership team. OmniVerse's business model combines upstream (35% of revenue), refining/petrochemicals (28%), retail/marketing (18%), power generation (12%) and renewables (7%). These units operate with different risk, economics, workforce and regulatory needs. Before selecting a solution, the team must establish the true business problem, the stakeholder map and measurable baseline.
**Discovery question:** What breaks across Explore → Extract → Refine → Transport → Distribute → Retail → Customer, and where do generation, dispatch and customer service fit?
[Read the Enterprise theme](https://github.com/nbnayak88/successlabs-case-studies/blob/main/01-energy/omniverse-energy-ltd/master/theme-a-enterprise.md).
## Act II — Choose the Future (Master 05–08)
CEO Asha Menon, CFO **Michael Reed**, COO **Farah Al-Hassan** and sustainability executive **Leena Fernandes** weigh three strategies: local pilots, a centralized platform-first program, or federated value-stream transformation under common standards. The provisional learning-case preference is the third option, but only if investment, safety and adoption gates can be demonstrated.
**The North Star:** A connected, resilient, customer-centric energy enterprise delivering operational excellence and a credible energy transition.
[Read the Strategy theme](https://github.com/nbnayak88/successlabs-case-studies/blob/main/01-energy/omniverse-energy-ltd/master/theme-b-strategy.md).
## Act III — Architect the Enterprise (Master 09–12)
The business architecture links **Resource-to-Energy, Asset-to-Reliability, Customer-to-Service and Insight-to-Decision** value streams to business capabilities, accountable processes, data, applications and technology. CDIO **Rajiv Kapoor** proposes governed Customer/Asset/Product/Supplier 360 and secure event-driven integration. CISO **Omar Haddad** insists that cloud-connected intelligence must never override critical OT safety boundaries. Enterprise architect **Hannah Fischer** asks which capabilities and outcomes justify each design decision.
[Read the Architecture theme](https://github.com/nbnayak88/successlabs-case-studies/blob/main/01-energy/omniverse-energy-ltd/master/theme-c-architecture.md).
## Act IV — Fund and Sequence (Master 13–16)
The original fictional case proposes **USD 900 million over 36 months** across infrastructure, data and analytics, digital experience, cybersecurity and change. It lists **USD 635 million in annual benefit categories**, but these are unvalidated run-rate assumptions, not realized net cash flow. The source also provides NPV figures without the cash-flow and discount-rate evidence needed for independent verification.
**Year 1:** stabilize, standardize and pilot. **Year 2:** integrate and scale. **Year 3:** optimize and transform. CFO Michael Reed will not approve a full rollout without credible downside scenarios, benefits ownership and financial evidence.
[Read the Transformation theme](https://github.com/nbnayak88/successlabs-case-studies/blob/main/01-energy/omniverse-energy-ltd/master/theme-d-transformation.md).
## Act V — Make Change Safe and Human (Master 17–19)
Refinery operations manager **Meera Iyer** demands safe cutover and rollback. CHRO **Nina Kohli** raises workforce resilience and adoption. Global Benefits Director **Dennis Abbott** asks how enrollment changes will reconcile with payroll. Rewards Lead **Priyanka Rao** asks whether variable-pay measures encourage safety and fairness. The team learns that cybersecurity, plant operations, HR, procurement and change management are inseparable from transformation success.
[Read the Execution theme](https://github.com/nbnayak88/successlabs-case-studies/blob/main/01-energy/omniverse-energy-ltd/master/theme-e-execution.md).
## Act VI — Prove Value (Master 20–22)
The fictional case sets Year 3 targets: OEE **72% → 85%**, unplanned downtime **8% → 2%**, NPS **35 → 65**, digital channel adoption **25% → 85%**, and digital services revenue **2% → 15%**. None is an achieved result. The board must decide whether the evidence justifies scaling, redesigning or stopping initiatives.
[Read the Value theme](https://github.com/nbnayak88/successlabs-case-studies/blob/main/01-energy/omniverse-energy-ltd/master/theme-f-value.md).
