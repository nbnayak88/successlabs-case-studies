# Hero Story — The Batch That Lost Its Passport

> **GICS02 · Fictional educational case · v 1.0 · 2026-10-19.** Source figures are fictional baselines or targets. New names, incidents, options and exercises are illustrative; no achieved outcomes or approved implementation are claimed.

## Prologue — One shipment, three versions of the truth
An industrial customer asks why a material certificate differs from its shipment record. A laboratory correction has not reached the customer portal. The plant also sees a reliability warning, while procurement cannot reconcile a recycled-feedstock certificate. These events are invented discovery triggers.

## Act I — Understand the enterprise
CEO Ananya Deshpande convenes operations, finance, commercial, workforce and sustainability leaders. Their fictional business spans 31 countries and five materially different segments. The question is not which dashboard to buy: which broken handoff causes which business risk? (OMF-M01–04)

## Act II — Choose the future
CFO Thomas van Dijk challenges a USD540 M proposed envelope. COO Kavita Rao demands safe plant authority. Digital and Sustainability Officer Sofia de Vries asks for auditable material and carbon lineage. Compare local pilots, enterprise platforms first and federated value-stream pilots. The provisional third choice must survive downside and readiness tests. (OMF-M05–08)

## Act III — Architect the digital thread
Architect Priya Nair maps Explore→Extract→Process→Manufacture→Distribute→Recover→Reuse. Quality lead Rohan Kulkarni insists that lab corrections, batch splits, blending and recycled inputs retain provenance. Data/AI lead Diego Alvarez asks who owns the golden record. CISO Hassan Al-Farsi refuses unsafe cloud dependence for critical controls. (OMF-M09–12)

## Act IV — Fund learning before scale
Finance separates recurring operating savings from working-capital release, tests overlap in USD350 M listed benefit categories and demands phased cash flow. Year 1 establishes governance and pilots; Year 2 integrates and scales selected use cases; Year 3 extends circularity and controlled optimization. (OMF-M13–16)

## Act V — Make change safe and human
Plant Director Elena Petrova requires rollback. CHRO Arjun Mehta and shift representative Nikhil Sen design accessible learning around shift coverage and critical certification. Customer Amelia Chen expects correction notifications, not just a prettier passport. (OMF-M17–19)

## Act VI — Prove or change the thesis
The pilot must measure OEE, downtime, yield, carbon lineage, recycled content and adoption without confusing targets with achievements. OEE83%, yield 97% and recycled content 40% are Year 3 aspirations. The simulation ends with a board decision supported by evidence; no successful historical rollout is invented. (OMF-M20–22)

**Your challenge:** What would you ask before recommending a solution, what would you test first, and what evidence would change your mind?
