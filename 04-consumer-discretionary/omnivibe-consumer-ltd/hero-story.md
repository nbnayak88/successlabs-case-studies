# Hero Story — The Promise the Store Could Not Keep

> **GICS04 · Fictional educational case · v1.0 · 2026-10-19.** Source figures are fictional enterprise baselines. Names, incidents, strategic options and non-workforce designs are illustrative learning extensions. No implemented client outcome, selected product capability or approved budget is claimed.

## Prologue
A fictional customer arrives to collect an order the app marked ready. A store associate cannot reconcile the promise and is still waiting for access and training from a disconnected onboarding handoff. These are invented triggers; their relationship must be investigated.

## Act I — Understand the enterprise
CEO Maya Sterling asks how one brand ecosystem can operate across six segments and48 countries while workforce information and experiences remain fragmented. The team separates source facts from assumed customer failures. (OVC-M01–04)

## Act II — Choose the future
Brand chief Elena Rossi protects distinct experiences; CFO Adrian Tan asks for actual baseline evidence. Compare brand-local pilots, platform-first centralization and federated journey experiments. No investment figure or quantified benefit has been supplied. (OVC-M05–08)

## Act III — Connect capability and architecture
CHRO Ravi Kapoor treats13 journeys as a connected workforce lifecycle. Architect Priya Shah and integration lead Jonas Lim trace capability, process, data and application boundaries. CISO Farah Ahmed challenges unnecessary employee-data sharing. Proposed SAP components require product and country validation. (OVC-M09–12)

## Act IV — Sequence and fund evidence
The source has four waves: foundation; talent lifecycle; capability/rewards; intelligence/experience. Finance requires pilot costs and measurable hypotheses rather than borrowed sector budgets. Brand/country scope and local controls must be agreed. (OVC-M13–16)

## Act V — Make the handoff work
Payroll lead Nadia Rahman refuses unreconciled data. Store director Oliver Brooks and associate Nikhil Das test shift-compatible practice. Learning lead Aisha Okafor asks for demonstrated service capability instead of course completion. (OVC-M17–19)

## Act VI — Learn from the result
The board reviews readiness, data quality, payroll accuracy, adoption and customer evidence. Baselines and targets remain TBD until measured. The case ends at an evidence-based scale/redesign/stop decision; no successful implementation is invented. (OVC-M20–22)

**Your challenge:** What would you ask first, what small experiment would expose a bad assumption, and what evidence would change your recommendation?
