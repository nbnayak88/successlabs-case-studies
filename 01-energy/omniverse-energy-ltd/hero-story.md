# Hero Story — The Night the Energy System Stopped Talking

> **GICS01 · Fictional educational case · v1.0 (2026-10-19).** Figures from the supplied Notion case are fictional scenario baselines or targets. New characters and incidents are illustrative. No real client or employee is represented.

## Prologue — A night shift with three unanswered questions
At a fictional coastal refinery, a critical rotating asset shows signs of degradation. The maintenance system knows the last work order; the control room sees current alarms; procurement knows that a replacement part is delayed. No one has a trusted end-to-end view. At the same time, a major industrial customer challenges the accuracy of its consumption and outage notifications, while a renewable operations team cannot reconcile generation forecasts with dispatch planning.

These incidents are **invented learning triggers**, not historical events.

## Act I — The CEO's dilemma
CEO **Dr. Asha Menon** convenes a crisis and transformation meeting. OmniVerse is a fictional USD 85B energy enterprise operating across upstream, refining, retail, power and renewables. Its 68,000 employees and extensive asset footprint support reliable energy delivery—but functional silos and inconsistent information make improvement difficult. The board expects operational reliability, a credible energy transition and financial discipline.

**Opening challenge:** “We cannot afford to make energy operations less safe while making them more digital. What should change first?”

## Act II — The strategic fork
CFO **Michael Reed** sees a proposed USD 900M three-year transformation budget. COO **Farah Al-Hassan** insists on operational safety. Sustainability executive **Leena Fernandes** demands auditable emissions data. Chief Digital and Information Officer **Rajiv Kapoor** argues for a reusable enterprise foundation.

Three choices emerge:
- **Option A: Business-unit pilots first.** Fast local learning; higher fragmentation risk.
- **Option B: Central platform first.** Strong common architecture; slower benefits and adoption risk.
- **Option C: Federated, value-stream-led transformation.** Common standards with controlled business-unit pilots and outcome ownership.

**Provisional recommendation:** C, with formal safety, data, cyber, adoption and benefits gates. This is a simulation recommendation, not an approved historical decision.

## Act III — Architect the energy value chain
The enterprise maps **Explore → Extract → Refine → Transport → Distribute → Retail → Customer**, plus **Generate → Dispatch → Serve** for power and renewables. Four cross-enterprise value streams organize the change: **Resource-to-Energy**, **Asset-to-Reliability**, **Customer-to-Service**, and **Insight-to-Decision**.

The transformation is not a single software implementation. It requires business capabilities, operating model, enterprise data governance, secure OT/IT integration, digital channels and accountable decisions.

## Act IV — The human challenge
At the refinery, **Meera Iyer** refuses a cutover without a safe rollback. CHRO **Nina Kohli** asks how scarce safety-critical skills will be retained. Benefits leader **Dennis Abbott** warns that employee benefits changes must reach payroll correctly. Rewards lead **Priyanka Rao** challenges whether variable-pay goals will encourage safe performance or the wrong behavior.

**Lesson:** Workforce design, compensation, benefits, change adoption and industrial safety belong in the enterprise transformation—not as afterthoughts.

## Act V — The investment committee
The original Notion case provides USD 900M in proposed three-year investment categories and USD 635M in stated annual benefit categories. It also lists 3-year NPV estimates that cannot be accepted without an explicit discount rate, cash-flow schedule and cost/benefit assumptions.

CFO Michael Reed asks: “Which benefits are incremental? Which are overlapping? Who will validate the baseline? What happens in the downside case?”

## Act VI — The proof
The fictional Year 3 targets include OEE 85% (baseline 72%), unplanned downtime 2% (baseline 8%), NPS 65 (baseline 35), digital channel adoption 85% (baseline 25%), and digital services revenue 15% (baseline 2%). These are **targets, not achieved results**.

CEO Asha Menon will approve scale only when pilots demonstrate safer operations, trusted data, workforce adoption, customer improvement and auditable economic value.

## Your mission
You are the Lead Business Transformation Architect. Lead the first discovery meeting, map business capabilities, elicit requirements, compare strategic options and defend a staged investment proposal.

**Yoodli opening question (CEO):** “Our energy business has survived decades of change. Why should we believe another transformation program will create real value rather than more platforms?”
