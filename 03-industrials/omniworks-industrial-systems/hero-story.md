# The OmniWorks Hero Story — One Industrial Enterprise, One Connected Future

**Story ID:** OW-STORY-001 · **Status:** Fictional simulation narrative

## Act I — The promise
For five decades, OmniWorks built trusted equipment for aerospace, mobility, factories and infrastructure. Its strength was engineering excellence; its weakness was that the company operated as a collection of product and functional silos. Product designers, factory teams, field engineers, customers and suppliers rarely saw the same asset history.

**Protagonist:** **Dr. Maya Richter**, fictional Group CEO, faces an inflection point. Hardware margins are under pressure while customers increasingly buy guaranteed uptime, rapid service and verifiable lifecycle data—not merely machines.

## Act II — The disruption
On a Monday morning, **Elena Brooks**, a fictional major transport customer, escalates repeated asset failures. A field technician can see alarms, but cannot reliably join telemetry to product configuration, supplier batch, warranty terms and maintenance history. An aerospace customer simultaneously asks for secure digital traceability as a condition for renewal. The board questions whether a USD 575M three-year investment can deliver value without creating more disconnected platforms.

These are **invented incidents**, designed to trigger role-play and architectural decisions.

## Act III — The difficult choice
The executive team debates three strategies:
- **A. Local lighthouse acceleration:** rapid pilots at selected sites, but fragmentation risk.
- **B. Enterprise platform first:** strong consistency, but slower early benefits.
- **C. Federated value-stream transformation:** shared enterprise standards with staged business-owned delivery.

**Recommended simulation position:** Option C, subject to financial, cybersecurity, operational safety and adoption gates. This is a case recommendation, not a recorded board decision.

## Act IV — The transformation
Four value streams drive the redesign:
1. **Engineer-to-Product** — requirements, product design, configuration, quality and certification.
2. **Plan-to-Operate** — supply, manufacturing, maintenance, energy and quality.
3. **Asset-to-Outcome** — telemetry, service, uptime and performance-based commercial models.
4. **Insight-to-Decision** — governed data, AI, scenario intelligence and executive decisions.

The critical architectural move is a **digital thread** connecting PLM → ERP/MES → EAM/FSM → customer and supplier touchpoints. Plant OT remains safety-led, segmented and resilient.

## Act V — The proof
The executive council will only call this transformation successful when evidence shows better equipment effectiveness, reduced unplanned downtime, improved first-time fix, higher service revenue mix and lower energy intensity. **Targets are not achieved results.** Benefits must be independently validated against a controlled baseline.

## Act VI — The next horizon
If the company proves repeatable value, it can expand connected services, digital twins and product-led ecosystems. If pilots fail, the team must choose what to stop, redesign or scale—not hide the failure.

## Hero's challenge for learners
You are the **Lead Transformation Architect**. In a client discovery meeting, determine what happened, what evidence is missing, who owns the problem, which option is defensible, and how you would prove value without compromising safety.
